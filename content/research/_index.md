## My ongoing research is about the role of statisticians in policymaking.

I focus on the method of meta-analysis, which was designed to resolve scientific disagreements but has increasingly been used to mediate social conflict. I introduce the concept of "*p*-value politics" to theorize how a reliance on statistical tools to settle debate often supplants more inclusive processes of deliberation.

### I study three cases, the first of which is hospital surgery departments

My recent [*Sociology of Health & Illness* paper](/research/peer-reviewed-papers/shi/) stemming from this case study argues that meta-analysts play a moral role in the hospital, intervening to settle debate over what counts as a "good" outcome from surgery.

### My second empirical case are "knowledge brokers" in global health.

Knowledge brokers use meta-analysis to resolve conflicts over where aid money should be allocated. As centralized bodies like the WHO lose funding, I argue, meta-analysts are empowered to choose which local NGOs are worthy of funding.

### I approach my third case---the US federal courts---with mixed methods.

At the 2025 Law and Society Association Annual Meeting, I presented a quantitative analysis of more than 500 court documents related to meta-analysis. (The cases in my dataset are [visualized here](/research/data/legalcitationnetwork/).) I found that judges clash with meta-analysts because the latter seek to resolve disputes over the facts of cases outside of court.

## My earlier work focused on health social movements.

My 2023 [*Social Studies of Science* paper](/research/peer-reviewed-papers/3s2/), follows a group of people with type 1 diabetes as they engineer an open-source artificial pancreas. My digital ethnography of the community filled an important gap in the science & technology studies (STS) literature by showing how lay expertise can be "routinized" over time.

An earlier research project studied how contemporary HIV activists engaged with clinical trials of the novel HIV prevention drug PrEP, also culminating in a [*Social Studies of Science* paper](/research/peer-reviewed-papers/3s1/).

In collaboration with Christine Percheski, I am revisiting HIV PrEP in a quantitative study of the drug's accessibility. Percheski and I introduce a measure of PrEP uptake that better captures variation in rural US counties where data are sparse. I presented this project at the [Interdisciplinary Association for Population Health Science Conference in 2023](/research/data/prepproject/). Our novel measure is depicted [here as an interactive map](/research/data/prepproject/).
